1. What Are Executive Regulations?

Executive regulations are the detailed decisions and rules issued by the Kuwait Environment Public Authority (KEPA) to apply Environmental Protection Law No. 42 of 2014. The law sets the general framework, while regulations specify operational details: how to apply, required documents, permitted emission limits, etc.

There are 17 main executive regulations issued by KEPA, covering various environmental aspects: air, water, waste, noise, radiation, biodiversity, laboratories, and more.

💡 Law vs. Regulations

Law = General framework issued by the National Assembly. Executive Regulations = Practical details issued by KEPA. The law says "air must be protected"; the regulation specifies "the maximum sulfur dioxide emission limit is X ppm".

3. Environmental Project Classification

Executive regulations classify projects into three categories with different requirements:

Class A — High Impact

  • Refineries and petrochemical complexes.
  • Large power plants.
  • Chemical factories.
  • Landfills.
  • Large fish farms.
  • Major marine projects.

Requirements: A Scoping Report first, before preparing the study, then a full EIA + EMP + public consultation + Supreme Council approval.

💡 The Scoping Report is a critical first step in Class A — it defines the study scope and key environmental aspects to cover, and must be approved by KEPA before starting.

Class B — Medium Impact

  • Large hotels and hospitals.
  • Mid-size factories.
  • Wastewater treatment plants.
  • Large warehouses.
  • Petrol stations.
  • Major real-estate projects.

Requirements: Environmental reports + EMP + technical approval.

Class C — Low Impact

  • Small industrial workshops with limited environmental impact.
  • Small clinics and medical laboratories.
  • Car wash stations.
  • Small metal-forming workshops.
  • Facilities with limited environmental impact.

Requirements: Environmental form or permit + basic precautionary conditions.

💡 Important Note

Regular retail shops, restaurants, cafes, salons, and offices do not require environmental approval from KEPA. They only need municipal and health licenses. Environmental approval is only required for facilities with actual environmental impact.

4. Steps to Obtain Environmental Approval

Obtaining KEPA approval follows 7 main steps:

  1. Initial inquiry — Determine the project's environmental classification.
  2. Application submission — With the basic documents.
  3. Environmental study preparation — Per classification (EIA, reports, form/permit).
  4. Technical review — By KEPA's competent committees.
  5. Modifications (if needed) — In response to reviewer feedback.
  6. Initial approval — To start the project under conditions.
  7. Final approval — After verifying compliance with conditions.

❓ FAQ

How do I know my project's environmental classification?

Classification depends on activity type, size, and location. Contact GEC for an accurate, free 15-minute classification assessment.

Can I apply without an EIA study?

Only for Class C projects. Classes A and B require an EIA study. Class B requires environmental reports + EMP. Class A requires a Scoping Report + full EIA + EMP. Bypassing these requirements will result in immediate rejection.

What if my study is rejected?

Often rejection is due to data gaps or technical observations, and the study can be revised and resubmitted. If rejected entirely and you receive a violation, you can:

  1. Submit a grievance letter (كتاب تظلم) to KEPA.
  2. Request a settlement order (أمر صلح) for amicable resolution.
  3. If unresponsive, the case escalates to the competent court.

GEC handles grievances and settlement files professionally with strong technical support.

Do procedures differ for foreign companies?

No, procedures are the same for all companies in Kuwait. However, foreign companies typically need a local partner or agent for submission.

How much does an EIA cost in Kuwait?

Costs range from KD 1,500 to 25,000+ depending on classification and project size. Contact us for a detailed quote within 24 hours.
